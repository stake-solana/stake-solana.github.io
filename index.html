Solana staking:
how long does unstaking take?

Native unstaking follows epoch boundaries. An epoch lasts roughly 2–3 days; your wait depends on when you deactivate and the network's cooldown capacity.

  1. Request deactivation

    Sign the deactivation transaction for your native stake account. Your SOL enters cooldown.

    When you choose to exit

  2. Wait for the epoch boundary

    Stake becomes inactive at epoch transitions. High network-wide deactivation can extend the process across additional epochs.

    Usually the next boundary

  3. Withdraw to your wallet

    Once the stake is inactive, sign a withdrawal to receive the available SOL and accrued rewards.

    After cooldown completes

Need liquidity sooner?

A liquid staking token can be swapped for SOL when market liquidity is available. Compare the swap price and slippage with the pool's redemption value. Direct redemption follows the pool's withdrawal schedule and reserve availability.

Solana staking fees:
how do they affect returns?

  • Commission comes from rewardsA 5% validator commission takes 5% of earned rewards. At 6.5% gross APR, the rate after commission is 6.175%.
  • Pool fees apply in sequenceA 2% pool reward fee after that commission brings the net APR to 6.0515%. Use both fee fields to compare the same gross rate.
  • Keep transaction costs separateThe base network fee is 5,000 lamports (0.000005 SOL) per signature, plus any priority fee. Stake-account creation also needs a rent-exempt reserve, recoverable when the account is closed.

Is it safe to Stake Solana?
What to check

  • Keep control of your authoritiesFor native delegation, check the stake and withdrawal authority addresses. The withdrawal authority controls access to the account's SOL.
  • Look beyond the headline APRCompare validator uptime, voting performance, commission history, and stake concentration. A low commission matters alongside consistent operation.

Stake Solana: common questions

Rewards, wallet control, and access to your SOL, explained before you stake.

What is the difference between SOL staking APR and APY?

APR expresses the annual reward rate before compounding. APY includes the effect of reinvesting rewards. Native staking rewards join the stake balance, so future rewards can accrue on that larger balance. Compare rates using the same fee and compounding basis.

How much SOL do I need to start staking?

The minimum depends on the wallet, validator route, or liquid staking pool you select. For native staking, budget for the delegated amount and the stake account's rent-exempt reserve. Keep a separate SOL balance for transaction fees.

When does native SOL staking start earning rewards?

New delegations first activate at an epoch boundary. Rewards accrue on active stake and are distributed by epoch. Activation may span additional epochs when network-wide stake changes reach the protocol's capacity.

Can I use my SOL while it is staked?

Native SOL remains in its stake account until deactivation and withdrawal. Liquid staking gives you a transferable token that supported applications can accept. Swapping or using that token introduces the relevant trading, liquidity, and application risks.

Is a liquid staking token always worth one SOL?

Each pool defines its token-to-SOL redemption rate. For tokens that accrue rewards through exchange-rate growth, one token can represent more than one SOL over time. The market swap price can differ from the pool's redemption value.

Can I split my SOL across several validators?

Yes. Each native stake account delegates to one validator, so use separate accounts to spread your SOL across several validators. A liquid staking pool manages its validator allocation according to its own strategy.

What are the risks of staking Solana?

SOL's market price can fall, validator performance can reduce rewards, and native cooldown delays access to funds. Liquid staking adds smart contract, pool management, and token liquidity risks. Protect your wallet keys and review each transaction before signing.
